▼ 📐 Construction Factor (Ci)

📖 Which class should I pick?
Match your Page 4 Construction Type to ISO Class:
IBC Construction TypeISO ClassF FactorDescription
Type V — Wood FrameClass 11.5Wood frame walls, floors, and roof. Highest fire spread.
Type III — OrdinaryClass 21.0Masonry/brick exterior walls, wood interior framing.
Type II — Non-CombustibleClass 3 or 40.8Steel frame, metal panels. No fire rating required (Class 3) or masonry exterior (Class 4).
Type IV — Heavy TimberClass 21.0Mass timber / large dimensional lumber. Treated like joisted masonry.
Type I — Fire ResistiveClass 5 or 60.6Concrete/steel, 2+ hr rated (Class 5 = modified, Class 6 = full).

Effective Area: Auto-loads from Page 4 "Total Sq Ft". For multi-story buildings with fire separations on each floor, use area per floor. Without separations, use total building area.

▼ 🏭 Occupancy Factor (Oi)

📖 Which combustibility class?
Match what's INSIDE the building:
ClassFactorExamples
C-1 Non-Combustible0.75Steel/concrete products, empty buildings, parking garages, masonry warehouses with non-combustible contents
C-2 Limited Combustible0.85Metal furniture offices, restaurants with commercial kitchen, churches with wood pews, schools
C-3 Combustible1.00Most retail stores, apartments, hotels, wood furniture, paper/office supplies — this is the default/most common
C-4 Free Burning1.15Upholstered furniture stores, clothing/textile stores, fast food restaurants with lots of grease, tire shops
C-5 Rapid Burning1.25Flammable liquid storage, fireworks, paint stores, foam/plastic manufacturing, sawmills, hay/straw storage

When in doubt, use C-3 (1.00). Most commercial buildings fall here.

Communication (P) — internal connected spaces:

Connection TypeSuggested P Value
No shared walls or connected buildings0.00
Shared fire wall with fire doors0.05–0.10
Open communication (shared attic, connected strip mall)0.15–0.25

Combined max (X + P) = 0.75. For standalone buildings with nothing nearby, leave both at 0.
